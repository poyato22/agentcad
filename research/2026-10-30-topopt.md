# D14: topology optimization and generative design for a part

*Research and feasibility spike, 2026-10-30. Spike code: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-hartigan-Documents-Claude-Projects/8b6e121f-d432-4043-912f-dff97e8094a0/scratchpad/topopt-spike/spike.py` (scratch, not in the repo).*

**Bottom line.** Build D14 in-house as a **3D SIMP voxel optimizer in numpy/scipy**. It uses a structured hex grid, a geometric-multigrid-preconditioned CG solver, a density filter and an OC update. Run it in a sandboxed worker, turn the density field into geometry with **surface nets + Taubin smoothing**, and build the result directly into an **OCCT solid with shared topology**. Then re-impose the exact design-space faces and keep-out bores with **transversal B-rep booleans**, and verify with the existing **gmsh + CalculiX** stack. The spike does all of this end to end with **no new dependencies**. On the 60×40×20 mm cantilever bracket at 1 mm voxels (48,000 elements, half-symmetric model), it **converges in 34 iterations / ~31 s**. Geometry takes ~15 s and produces **one valid build123d `Solid` at 19.8% volume** that re-imports from STEP and takes further booleans. CalculiX on the final solid gives a compliance of **149.3 N·mm against the optimizer's 150.5 N·mm (−0.8%)**. No existing library is a better base: the good ones are MIT but solver-bound to x86 MKL or written for 2D, and the CalculiX-native ones are LGPL/GPL and FreeCAD-shaped.

## 2. Density field to a usable solid

**Iso-surface.** Element densities are averaged to nodes, and the field is padded with a void shell so the surface always closes. Two extractors were tried, both vectorized numpy with no tables:

- **Marching tetrahedra** (6-tet Kuhn split, vertices welded by global edge key). Watertight, but **69k triangles** for this bracket, with many slivers. The first version produced ~9k zero-area triangles where node values equal the 0.5 iso exactly (4 of 8 neighbours solid is common), and that made OCCT reject the edges.
- **Surface nets** (one vertex per sign-changing cell, one quad per sign-changing grid edge, split on the shorter diagonal). **19.8k triangles, watertight, 0 non-manifold edges or vertices, 12 ms.** Node values within 0.02 of the iso level are pushed off it, so neighbouring cell vertices can never coincide (minimum triangle area 0.013 mm²). This is the recommendation. scikit-image's Lewiner marching cubes would be the off-the-shelf alternative, but it is not needed.

**Smoothing.** Taubin λ|μ = 0.5|−0.53 with 10 iterations on the uniform Laplacian changes the volume by **+0.1%**. In an early run with degenerate triangles, the same filter lost 19%, which is why degeneracy has to be fixed before smoothing.

**Mesh to B-rep.** `BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing` is the textbook route but is slow and tolerance-driven. Sewing is not needed, because the mesh is already indexed. The spike builds one `TopoDS_Vertex` per mesh vertex and one shared `TopoDS_Edge` per mesh edge (reversed as needed). Each triangle becomes a `Geom_Plane` face whose normal is the outward triangle normal, bounded by a wire running counter-clockwise around it. The faces go into a shell and the shell into a solid via `BRep_Builder`. The result is **valid under `BRepCheck_Analyzer`, with exact volume, in 5.2 s for 19.8k faces**, and build123d accepts it as `Solid(...)` with `.is_valid == True`. (STL import in build123d gives a mesh face, not a solid you can do booleans on.)

**Size.** The final bracket has 12,491 planar faces after the booleans. Its **STEP is 31 MB** and STL 0.7 MB. STEP re-imports in 5.3 s, valid. That STEP is heavy but workable. Store the organic body as STL/3MF for printing and keep the STEP for CAD round-trips. Coarser voxels or a decimation pass would cut it 2–4×.

**Re-imposing keep-in/keep-out features exactly.** The naive approach is to fuse the original flange box and cut the holes. It took **131 s and failed** on the first attempt, because the organic surface is nearly coplanar with the keep-in faces, which is the worst case for OCCT booleans. The fix is to make every boolean **transversal** by shaping the density field first:
1. **Extend outward:** the outermost element layer is copied one element past the design-space boundary, so material that touches the boundary crosses it. `common(organic, design_space_brep)` then cuts it back to the **exact original planar faces** (the mounting face and the pad top). This took 2.9 s.
2. **Shrink keep-outs in the field:** each keep-out element takes the 3×3×3 max of its neighbours, so the field's hole is about one voxel smaller than the real one. `cut(part, keep_out_breps)` then cuts through solid material and leaves **exact cylindrical bores**. This took 1.2 s for 3 holes.
3. Keep-in regions are passive (density 1) during optimization, so they are already solid in the field. Their *functional* faces lie on the design-space boundary or on keep-out bores, and steps 1 and 2 make those exact. Interior faces of a keep-in region (such as the back of the flange) stay organic.

Downstream edits work on the result: an extra Ø4 through-cut on the re-imported STEP took 2.0 s and a fused backing plate 2.8 s, both valid, single solid.

## 3. Recommended pipeline and spike results

**Pipeline:**
1. Voxelize the design space and regions at h ≈ 1 mm.
2. Run SIMP: p = 3, Emin = 1e-4·E, density filter r = 1.5h, OC with move 0.2, stopping when the change in compliance is under 0.1% over 5 iterations or after 60 iterations. Use the optional symmetry half-model.
3. Each FE solve is CG preconditioned by a 3-level geometric multigrid ([Amir, Aage & Lazarov 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-013-1015-5)). The coarse operators are Galerkin (PᵀAP), smoothing is symmetric damped Jacobi (ω = 0.6, 3+3 sweeps), and the coarsest grid is solved directly with SuperLU. If a solve needs more than 80 CG iterations, the cycle falls back to 2 levels. CG tolerance is 1e-4, warm-started from the previous displacement.
4. Build the nodal field (extend + keep-out shrink), extract with surface nets, and apply Taubin.
5. Build the shared-topology OCCT solid, then `common` with the design space and `cut` the keep-outs.
6. Export STEP + STL.
7. Verify: weld the B-rep's STL at 1e-3 mm, mesh it to tet10 with gmsh in a child process (HXT, falling back to Delaunay), then run CalculiX on the same load case.

**Spike numbers.** The vf 0.20 / r 1.5 run predates the symmetric-V-cycle and fallback fixes; the other variants include some or all of them. (M5 Pro, 18 cores, shared with ~20 other sessions; load average was 25–33 during most runs, so wall times are noisy):

Where the time goes, and the levers if it has to be faster:
- The scipy sparse matvec is single-threaded, at 5 ms on 157k DOFs, and each V-cycle runs ~9 of them. The levers are a symmetry half-model (2×), a looser CG tolerance, an Emin floor (1e-3 halves CG iterations at a 1% change in compliance), and a matrix-free stencil matvec.
- Variants are independent single-threaded processes, so 4–6 run in parallel on an M-series chip.
- **Low volume fractions break the 3-level cycle.** At vf 0.12 the members get thinner than the 4 mm coarsest grid can represent, and MG-CG hit its 200-iteration cap on every solve (523 s for 50 iterations). Two fixes went into the spike. First, the V-cycle was made exactly symmetric (3 pre-sweeps and 3 post-sweeps, where it had been 4/3). Second, it falls back to a **2-level cycle** (direct SuperLU one level down) when a solve needs more than 80 CG iterations, which brings it to 19–25 iterations per solve. In production, start with 2 levels for the half model, pad the grid so a 4th level exists, or use pyamg smoothed aggregation (MIT, arm64 wheel).
- Verification meshing (gmsh on a 12k-face STL) is the slowest step. It can run concurrently with the next variant, and coarser tets (2 mm) are fine for a ranking pass.

Rejected for v1:
- **BESO with ccx in the loop**: ~10× slower per iteration, plus file I/O.
- **Native ccx sensitivities**: they cannot do densities.
- **MMA**: not needed for compliance with a single volume constraint, where OC is optimal. Write it from Svanberg 1987 when stress or displacement constraints arrive. The popular Python port (arjendeetman/GCMMA-MMA-Python) is **GPL-3.0**, so don't copy it.

## 4. Generative design: variants and ranking

A "generative study" is a grid or list of `TopoptSpec` deltas, each run as an independent sandboxed worker (parallel, capped by `mises.parallel`):

- **Volume fraction sweep** (e.g. 0.12 / 0.2 / 0.3) traces the mass–stiffness Pareto front. With a *stiffness target* (max deflection δ* or compliance C*), Mises interpolates vf from the sweep, runs one confirming solve, and reports the lightest variant that passes.
- **Filter radius / minimum member size**: r_min = 1.5h gives fine, truss-like parts, and r_min = 2.5–3h gives chunkier, more printable ones. Add Heaviside projection (β continuation 1 → 8) with robust eroded/dilated designs when a true minimum length scale is required.
- **Load cases**: several cases with weights (weighted compliance), or one variant per dominant case, plus an "envelope" variant.
- **Manufacturing constraints**, as filters in the density chain:
  - Symmetry: mirror, which also halves the solve.
  - Extrusion / 2.5-D for milling or laser-cut: average along an axis.
  - AM overhang along a build direction: Langelaar's layer-wise filter, as implemented in pyMOTO (MIT) ([Langelaar 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00158-016-1522-2), [front-propagation variant](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/overhang-control-based-on-front-propagation-in-3d-topology-optimi)).
  - Minimum wall: filter radius.
  - Keep a boundary skin: a passive outer layer.
- **Resolution**: a coarse 2 mm sweep for ideation (~5–10 s each), then a 1 mm final for the chosen candidates.

**Ranking.** Every variant is verified, so its badges come from CalculiX records, never from the optimizer. Each card shows:
- mass (g, from the B-rep volume × density)
- compliance and peak deflection at the load faces
- max von Mises away from the fixtures, via `results.peaks` (fixture peak reported separately), and the safety factor against yield × FDM knockdown
- stiffness/mass ratio
- printability: overhang area fraction from triangle normals against the build direction, a minimum-thickness estimate from a distance transform of the voxel field, and an optional slicer estimate from the existing `print/slicer.py`
- face count and STEP size

Ranking is Pareto on (mass, deflection) filtered by SF ≥ target, with ties broken on printability. `sim_compare` already compares studies, so variant cards reuse it.

On (mass, deflection) the last three are all Pareto-optimal. The stress filter removes vf 0.12, and the agent would recommend r 2.5 mm unless deflection dominates. It is also why every generative card must carry the CalculiX numbers, not the optimizer's.

## 5. Recommended module design

New package `core/src/mises/topopt/`. Everything is pure numpy/scipy/OCP, with no new dependencies. gmsh and ccx stay in child processes as today.

```
topopt/
  spec.py      # pydantic models (the MCP tool args)
  voxelize.py  # B-rep -> grid masks: design space, keep-in, keep-out, fixture/load nodes
  fe.py        # hex8 KE, Level (CSR pattern + bincount assembly), MGCG (Galerkin MG-PCG)
  filters.py   # density filter (sparse H), Heaviside/robust, symmetry, extrusion, overhang (AM)
  simp.py      # optimize(problem, opts, on_iter) -> DensityResult   (OC; MMA later)
  geometry.py  # nodal_field, surface_nets, taubin, mesh_to_solid, reimpose(), export
  worker.py    # child-process entry: spec JSON in, JSON-lines progress out, artifacts in job dir
  study.py     # generative study: expand variants, schedule workers, verify, rank (Pareto)
  tools.py     # MCP tools; register_topopt_tools(registry)
```

**Key functions:**

```python
# voxelize.py
def grid_for(design_space: Solid, h_mm: float, pad: int = 0) -> Grid            # nx,ny,nz,origin,h
def solid_mask(shape: Solid, grid: Grid) -> np.ndarray[bool]                    # element centres inside
#   (BRepMesh triangulation + vectorised ray parity per z-column; no per-point OCCT calls)
def face_nodes(part: Solid, face_ids: list[str], grid: Grid, tol: float) -> np.ndarray[int]
def face_band(part: Solid, face_ids, grid, thickness_mm) -> np.ndarray[bool]    # keep-in skin around faces

# fe.py
class MGCG:  # __init__(grid, nu, fixed_dofs, levels); set_density(E_e); solve(f, x0, tol) -> (u, iters)

# simp.py
@dataclass
class Problem: grid; active: bool[ne]; keep_in: bool[ne]; keep_out: bool[ne];
               fixed_dofs: bool[ndof]; loads: list[(f: ndarray, weight)]; material: Material
def optimize(p: Problem, *, objective="compliance", volume_fraction=0.25, penal=3.0,
             filter_radius_mm=1.5, emin_rel=1e-4, max_iter=60, ctol=1e-3,
             constraints: Manufacturing | None = None,
             on_iter: Callable[[int, float, float, np.ndarray], None] | None = None) -> DensityResult
#   DensityResult: x (ne,), compliance_gray, compliance_bw, vf_bw, iters, history

# geometry.py
def nodal_field(x, grid, keep_out, extend=True) -> np.ndarray
def surface_nets(F, h, origin, iso=0.5) -> (V, T)

def reimpose(organic: Solid, design_space: Solid, keep_out: list[Solid],
             keep_in_exact: list[Solid] = ()) -> Solid   # common + cut (+ fuse only if transversal)

```

`map_faces` matters. The functional faces (the fixed mounting face, the load pad, the bores) come out of the booleans as exact copies of the original surfaces, so they can be matched by surface type plus coincidence. The verification study then reuses the user's original `fix` / `load` face specs on the new part with no agent guesswork.

**Worker contract.** This follows `sim/`. The core writes `spec.json` into a job dir and runs `python -m mises.topopt.worker job_dir` via `run_sandboxed`, with no network, writes only inside the job dir, a memory watchdog and a timeout. The worker prints JSON lines (`{"it":12,"c":..,"vf":..,"preview":"iter_012.glb"}`). Surface nets runs in 12 ms, so a **live organic preview every ~5 iterations** is basically free and makes a good demo. Outputs are `density.npy`, `organic.stl`, `part.step`, `part.stl`, `preview.glb` and `report.json`.

**MCP tools** (registered like `register_sim_tools`; all mutate only a *new* part revision on a branch, never the source part):

```python
class Region(BaseModel):            # keep-in / keep-out
    kind: Literal["faces", "part", "box", "cylinder"]
    faces: list[FaceSpec] = []      # "faces": a band of `thickness_mm` around these faces stays solid
    thickness_mm: float = 2.0
    part_id: str | None = None      # "part": another solid (e.g. a clearance envelope)
    box: list[float] | None = None  # [x0,y0,z0,x1,y1,z1]
    cylinder: dict | None = None    # {center, axis, radius, length}

class TopoptLoadCase(BaseModel):
    name: str
    loads: list[LoadSpec]           # reuse sim.tools.LoadSpec (face, newtons, direction)
    weight: float = 1.0

class Manufacturing(BaseModel):
    symmetry: list[Literal["x", "y", "z"]] = []
    extrusion_axis: Literal["x", "y", "z"] | None = None
    overhang: dict | None = None        # {"build_dir": "z", "angle_deg": 45}
    min_member_mm: float | None = None  # -> filter radius / robust projection

class TopoptRunArgs(BaseModel):
    part_id: str
    design_space: Literal["part", "bbox"] | str = "part"   # or a part_id of an envelope solid
    keep: list[Region] = []          # keep-in
    keep_out: list[Region] = []      # holes for bolts, clearance envelopes
    fixtures: list[dict]             # same shape as static-study supports: {"kind":"fixed","face":"x-min"}
    load_cases: list[TopoptLoadCase]
    material: str | None = None
    objective: Literal["min_compliance", "min_mass"] = "min_compliance"
    volume_fraction: float = Field(0.25, gt=0.03, lt=0.8)   # min_compliance
    max_deflection_mm: float | None = None                   # min_mass target (or safety_factor)
    resolution_mm: float | None = None   # default: ~50k elements in the design space
    filter_radius_mm: float | None = None
    manufacturing: Manufacturing = Manufacturing()
    verify: bool = True              # run a static sim on the result with the same load cases
    reason: str | None = None

async def topopt_run(ctx, args: TopoptRunArgs) -> ToolOutput
#   -> {topopt_id, new_part_id, mass_g, volume_fraction, compliance, sim_id (if verify),
#       max_deflection_mm, max_von_mises_mpa, safety_factor, files{step,stl,glb}, warnings}

class TopoptGenerateArgs(TopoptRunArgs):
    variants: list[dict] = []        # explicit deltas, e.g. [{"volume_fraction":0.15}, {"manufacturing":{...}}]
    sweep: dict | None = None        # or a grid: {"volume_fraction":[0.12,0.2,0.3], "filter_radius_mm":[1.5,3]}
    max_variants: int = Field(6, le=12)
    rank_by: list[str] = ["mass_g", "max_deflection_mm"]   # Pareto axes; SF >= target filters
async def topopt_generate(ctx, args) -> ToolOutput   # -> {study_id, variants:[...cards], pareto:[ids]}

async def topopt_status(ctx, topopt_id)   # progress + latest preview
async def topopt_result(ctx, topopt_id)   # full report
async def topopt_cancel(ctx, topopt_id)
async def topopt_accept(ctx, topopt_id, variant)  # promote the variant's part to a revision on the branch
```

Gate and approvals: `topopt_run` / `topopt_generate` are compute-only and write new parts on a branch, so they need no approval beyond the existing budget/parallel gate. `topopt_accept` goes through the normal edit approval.

- surface_nets watertight + manifold on random smoothed fields.
- `mesh_to_solid` valid and volume-exact.
- reimpose keeps the exact planar and cylindrical faces (`map_faces`).
- End to end on the bracket, marked `slow`: CalculiX compliance within 5% of the voxel black-white compliance. The spike gets 0.8%.

## 6. Risks and open items

- **STEP size** is 31 MB for 12k faces. Options: a decimation pass (quadric edge collapse in numpy, or manifold3d if a dependency is ever acceptable), or keeping STEP optional with STL/3MF as primary for printing. Onshape upload of a 31 MB STEP is untested.
- **The existing `mesh_step` path** (gmsh OCC on the STEP) was not tried on a 12k-face solid. The spike used the discrete-STL path, which took 35 s under load. Add an STL-source option to `mesh_worker` for topopt results.
- **Design spaces that are not boxes** are untested: they need `solid_mask` via ray parity and the `extend` trick on curved boundaries. For non-planar design boundaries, the `common` is still transversal, because the field crosses the boundary.
- **Thin members at low volume fractions** (vf ≤ 0.12) are both a solver problem and an accuracy problem. The solver is fixed by the 2-level fallback. For accuracy, enforce r_min ≥ 2–2.5h and refine automatically (see §4).
- **The OCCT STL export of the boolean result can carry near-duplicate vertices along seams.** Before it was welded at 1e-3 mm, the vf 0.12 export had 3–5 open edges and gmsh HXT failed ("No tetrahedra in region 1"). The spike now welds before meshing and falls back from HXT to the Delaunay mesher. Production should verify through the same welded-STL path, or through gmsh OCC on the STEP once that is measured.
- **Stress-constrained or min-mass-at-stress objectives** need MMA plus p-norm stress aggregation. They are not in v1. Stress is checked after the fact by CalculiX.
