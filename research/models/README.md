# Arm Mises agents with measurements, not models

Mises should not ship a CAD-generating model, a world model or a physics surrogate in v1. The best way to make its agents better at CAD is to surround the frontier agents users already bring with exact, kernel-derived tools, and to add only one or two small local models where frontier models are measurably weak. The evidence points the same way across all five model families. In 2026 benchmarks, general frontier models beat every fine-tuned open CAD-code model. The strongest open CAD models are licensed non-commercial anyway. Agent harnesses that feed back exact OpenCASCADE measurements raise median IoU from about 0.81 to 0.96. Vision-language models score roughly 25–30 points higher when they get the CAD code than when they get only renders. No world model or pretrained surrogate predicts stress, deflection or modes for an arbitrary printed part. So the adopt-now list is mostly tooling: a build123d-mcp-style measure-and-validate vocabulary, a labelled multi-view render bundle sent alongside the script and feature text, a typed requirements schema, honest CalculiX error bands, and a regression suite built on CC-BY data. The design-for-later list is a set of cheap seams: a model-backend interface on `mises` tools, a `fidelity_class` on every sim result, a solver-neutral environment file, and opt-in capture of approved merges and sim runs. Together these let a small, cleanly licensed sketch reader, a per-study surrogate, or a MuJoCo shake test be added without breaking the "verdicts come from records" rule. Everything in the free tier runs locally or on the user's own agent key, which keeps the developer's per-user cost at zero.

## Frontier agents with kernel feedback beat every specialist CAD model

The case for bringing your own frontier agent rather than shipping a CAD model is now quantitative. On **Text2CAD-Bench**, GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Sonnet, Gemini 3 Flash and DeepSeek-V3.2 reach Chamfer distances of 44–67 (×10³) on easy prompts. The fine-tuned specialists Text2CAD, CADFusion and Text2CADQuery sit at 219–229, with IoU of 0.03–0.08 against the frontier models' 0.44–0.59. The authors also find that CadQuery code beats DeepCAD-style command sequences on both validity and accuracy ([Text2CAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18430v1)). Absolute quality is still poor, though. On **CADEngBench**, which uses the same gmsh + CalculiX stack as Mises, only 1,030 of 2,400 generated programs execute, only 432 also meet requirements, and about **58% of executable outputs break an engineering or DFM rule**. No model leads throughout the benchmark ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)). When the same benchmark compares editing with generation, it finds that **1,071 item–model pairs pass only editing against 231 that pass only generation (4.64×)**. It also finds that edits to histories with joins, cuts or multiple bodies pass only 40–46% of the time ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)). That is strong support for Mises's edit-centric loop of Variable Studio changes plus simple feature additions on a branch, and for its nudge toward variable-driven parts.

The gains come from tools and feedback, not from new weights. **CADSmith** wraps an LLM in an execution-repair loop plus exact OpenCASCADE checks (bbox, volume, solid validity) and a separate VLM judge. It lifts execution from 95% to 100%, median IoU from 0.8085 to 0.9629, and mean Chamfer from 28.37 to 0.74, with no fine-tuning ([CADSmith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26512)). The Apache-2.0 **build123d-mcp** server reports raising the same model's CADGenBench score from 0.360 to 0.457, and validity from 88% to 100%, just by exposing render, measure, hole-finding, printability and compare tools ([build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)). That claim is self-reported by the tool's author. One aggregator puts build123d-mcp with Claude Opus 5 at the top of CADGenBench at 59.6%, but its own table conflicts with that, so the official leaderboard should be checked before quoting it ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/cadgenbenchgeneration); [CADGenBench Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingAI4Engineering/CADGenBench)). The sobering counterpoint is the FEA-feedback study: **Codex (GPT-5.5) and Claude Code (Opus 4.7) produced zero strict-passing assemblies on the first attempt**. The best configuration met about 20% of typed requirements. A text blueprint schema plus a 21-view renderer raised Box-IoU by about 0.1 ([Self-Improving CAD Agents with FEA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17448)). Agents get better with scaffolding, and they still cannot be trusted without it.

The specialist models also fail on licence before they fail on quality. **CAD-Recode** and **cadrille**, the state of the art for point cloud or images to CadQuery, are CC BY-NC 4.0 ([CAD-Recode LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/filaPro/cad-recode/main/LICENSE.md); [cadrille on HF](https://huggingface.co/maksimko123/cadrille)). **BrepGen** forbids commercial use of its code, data and weights ([BrepGen LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/samxuxiang/BrepGen/main/LICENSE)). Text2CAD is CC BY-NC-SA ([Text2CAD LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SadilKhan/Text2CAD/main/LICENSE)). The only commercially licensed open generators found are text-to-CadQuery **CAD-Coder** (Apache-2.0, Qwen2.5-7B) and **CADFusion** (MIT) ([CAD-Coder on HF](https://huggingface.co/gudo7208/CAD-Coder); [CADFusion LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/CADFusion/main/LICENSE)). Both were trained on DeepCAD-derived data, which comes from public Onshape documents whose copyright stays with their creators ([ABC record](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/44320?mode=full)). Neither model emits build123d, FeatureScript or Onshape feature JSON, and no model found anywhere produces named Variable Studio variables. On output format, the order of how directly an output maps onto Onshape is: FeatureScript, then CadQuery/build123d code, then sketch-and-extrude sequences, then Zoo's KCL, then dumb B-rep or mesh solids.

Two vendor paths deserve a specific verdict. Onshape's **FeatureScript MCP Server** (early access, August 2026) lets any LLM write, run and revise custom FeatureScript features from a remote endpoint ([PTC release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/ptc-advances-leadership-ai-driven-design-launch-onshape-featurescript-mcp-server-2026); [Onshape blog](https://www.onshape.com/de/blog/featurescript-mcp-server-enables-text-code-cad)). It is the most Onshape-native AI path and fits Mises's BYO-agent model. But it writes to documents outside the `mises` ledger and approval gates, and its allowance treatment is undocumented. Mises should therefore tell users about it as an optional sibling server, scoped to authoring reusable feature tools, and should never route its own geometry edits through it. **Zoo's Zookeeper** is a closed agent over its own KCL language at **$0.50/min** after free credit ([Zoo blog](https://zoo.dev/blog/announcing-zookeeper)). It has no Onshape bridge, so Mises should skip it as a component but use its price as an anchor for hosted agent credits. Zookeeper's default four-view "vision snapshot" self-review also confirms that render-and-check loops are now table stakes.

## Vision models critique intent but cannot verify geometry

Frontier VLMs are poor at reading CAD from pictures alone. On **BenchCAD**'s numeric QA over CadQuery renders (counts of teeth, chamfers, ratios), Gemini 3.1 Pro scores 0.587, Claude Opus 4.7 0.526 to 0.530 and GPT-5.3 0.514. OpenAI o3 scores 0.327, **below a blank-image baseline of 0.375**. Given the CAD code instead, the top models reach **0.82–0.84** ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1)). The failures are the ones that matter for Mises: missed threads and chamfers, wrong workplane or extrude direction, dropped operations, sweeps flattened into extrusions. On 2D mechanical drawings (**MechVQA**), the best models read dimensions and annotations at 85–88% but manage projection and multi-view reasoning at only 54–62%. The paper names a failure mode Mises must design against: "answers that look numerically plausible but violate geometric constraints" ([MechVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1)). Anthropic's own documentation says Claude's localization and counting are approximate and should not be used for tasks that need perfect precision without oversight ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).

The design consequence is a strict division of labour. Hole counts, diameters, wall thickness, clearances and interference come from OCCT and build123d topology queries, which are exact and free. The VLM, whether the user's agent or a local model, answers only "does this look like what was asked?" and catches gross errors such as a wrong extrude direction or a missing feature. Its verdicts render as unverified claims, never as "fits" or "verified" badges. Because code beats images by about 25–30 points, the cheapest perception upgrade is to always send structured text with the pictures. `mirror_render_views` should return a fixed bundle of iso plus three or more orthographic views at about 1,000–1,500 px, placed before the text and labelled "Image 1…", with edge lines and optional sections. Faces and holes should carry ID overlays that match `measure` output, so the model can refer to IDs instead of guessing coordinates. The bundle should also carry a text payload of mass, volume, bbox, hole list, feature list and the build123d script. This is a synthesis, not a tested recipe. The individual value of ID overlays and depth or normal maps is unmeasured, and the many-view gain is the one quantified piece ([FEA-feedback study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17448)). On Claude, a 1,000×1,000 render costs about **$5.18 per 1,000 images on Opus 5.5**, and the Files API avoids resending images every turn ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). That cost falls on the user's BYO key, not on Mises.

Render-and-critique loops are worth having but should not be oversold. The best-established result, **CADCodeVerify**, improved GPT-4 by about 7% in point-cloud distance and about 5% in compile or success rate ([CADCodeVerify](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05340v2)). Later systems run VLM critique only in early refinement rounds to save tokens ([Foundation Models for CAD Generation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05573)). Mises's tool descriptions should therefore push agents to call renders after each geometry change but lean on numeric returns for decisions. 3D-native encoders such as BrepCoder, BRepCLIP, PointLLM and ShapeLLM are research checkpoints with unverified licences ([BrepCoder](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22284); [BRepCLIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05515)). They add value only where Mises does *not* already have exact geometry, such as scan reverse-engineering or similarity search, so they belong in the later bucket.

A local critic for the free tier is feasible but tight. **Qwen3-VL-4B** in MLX peaks at about **3.6 GB** and generates about 33 tok/s on an M4 with 32 GB. **Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B** peaks at **17.5 GB** ([oMLX benchmarks](https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/kkmh7ej8), community-submitted). On a 24 GB laptop that is also running OCCT, gmsh, CalculiX and the UI, that means planning for a 4–8B model, loaded lazily and unloaded when idle. These figures were not measured on an M5 Pro. An Apache-2.0 MLX build of the 30B-A3B model exists ([HF](https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct-MLX-4bit/blob/main/README.md)), but each Qwen3-VL card's licence needs checking, because newer Qwen releases reportedly moved to more restrictive terms ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-gemma-4-apache-2-license-commercial-ai-deployment)). Gemma 4's move to Apache 2.0 makes it a clean fallback ([urandom.io](https://urandom.io/blog/2026-04-05-gemma-4-apache-license-matters-more-than-benchmarks)). Multimodal Llama's licence grant excludes EU-based companies, a trap for a distributor ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-releases-first-multimodal-llama-4-models-leaves-eu-out-in-the-cold/)).

## Sketch to part works as a staged pipeline, not an end-to-end model

Sketch to part (M9) is the one place where a specialist model clearly beats frontier models, but the specialists are still far from product-grade on real input. The best 2026 result, **HiCAD** (a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B), reaches 69.5% IoU on synthetic sketches but **43.3% on real volunteer sketches**. GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 and Claude 3.7 manage about 20% even on synthetic sketches, and cadrille falls to **17.7% IoU with 87% invalid outputs** on real ones ([HiCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15681)). Those baselines are older models, but the gap is large enough that "photo in, constrained part out with at most three questions" cannot rest on a single model call.

The pipeline that fits both the evidence and the design runs in four stages. First, take geometry from the strokes: pen input is already vectors, and photos get perspective-corrected and binarised before line and arc detection. Second, have a VLM read the handwritten dimensions on high-resolution crops, where 85%+ dimension reading is attainable ([MechVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1)). Third, infer constraints with snapping rules plus a sketch solver. Fourth, let the VLM propose intent (which number belongs to which line), let the solver check consistency, and turn the inconsistencies into the clarifying questions. Primitive and constraint inference research (PICASSO, Vitruvion, ViT set prediction) shows the stage-one and stage-three models are learnable, but none is product-grade ([PICASSO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13394); [Vitruvion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14124)). **CAD-Assistant**, a tool-augmented VLLM driving FreeCAD with a sketch parameterizer and cross-section tools, made its largest gains on autoconstraining and hand-drawn sketch parameterization. That supports the tool-staged approach over a monolithic one ([CAD-Assistant](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Mallis_CAD-Assistant_Tool-Augmented_VLLMs_as_Generic_CAD_Task_Solvers_ICCV_2025_paper.html)).

The most valuable future model investment sits in this stage, because small fine-tuned models win here decisively. **MechVL-4B-RL**, fine-tuned from Qwen3-VL-4B, scores **84.85** on MechVQA, above every closed model tested ([MechVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1)). An RL-trained **Qwen3-VL-2B scored 0.768** on BenchCAD image-to-code in-distribution, against about 0.40 for the best frontier model ([BenchCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1)). A 2–4B sketch reader fits on the laptop at zero marginal cost. But neither checkpoint has a stated weights licence, so Mises would need to train its own, on clean data. That means procedurally generated build123d parts rendered as synthetic sketches, BenchCAD (CC-BY-4.0, eval code MIT), and opt-in user sketches. DeepCAD, ABC, Text2CAD, Fusion 360 Gallery and CAD-Recode corpora are out without legal review ([Fusion 360 Gallery LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset/master/LICENSE.md); [CAD-Recode dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/filapro/cad-recode-v1.5)). For formal drawings, **eDOCr2** (MIT, 93.75% text recall) is the commercially safe open parser ([eDOCr2](https://github.com/javvi51/edocr2)). **Werk24** starts at **€490/month** ([Werk24](https://werk24.io/)), which rules it out for the free tier and leaves it, at most, as a paid-tier import.

## Physics surrogates, world models and 3D generators fail the engineering-units test

No learned model available in October 2026 can stand in for CalculiX on a user's part. Published structural surrogates are trained on one family at a time (brackets, plates, beams). When shapes leave that distribution, the error rises from **about 9% median to more than 40%** ([GNN surrogate generalization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.00557)). Even state-of-the-art 3D operators on their home automotive benchmarks sit at about **9–16% relative L2** field error ([CarBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.07847); [RETO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00062)). Field-average metrics also hide errors at the peak-stress hotspot, which is exactly the number Mises reports. The "foundation models" that exist are domain-locked: car aero (DoMINO NIM), centrifugal pumps, buildings (SeisGPT) ([PhysicsNeMo release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/physicsnemo/26.05/release-notes/index.html); [SeisGPT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75508-5)). Ansys SimAI, Siemens Simcenter PhysicsAI, PhysicsX and Neural Concept are quote-based enterprise platforms that train on the customer's own simulation data ([Ansys SimAI](https://ansys.com/products/simai); [Siemens](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/simcenter/whats-new-in-simcenter-physicsai-2026-1/)). None fits a free tier or embedding in a third-party product. The only shippable toolkit is **NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo** (Apache-2.0, with a small-deformation structural MeshGraphNet recipe). The NGC container carries a separate EULA, so it should be installed from pip or source ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/physics-ml-platform-physicsnemo-is-now-open-source)).

The right v1 move is to make CalculiX "live" through engineering rather than ML. That means a coarse second-order mesh first, cached meshes, reused factorization when only loads or materials change across the environment × material matrix, and the change in peak stress between coarse and refined runs shown as the result's error band. This costs nothing per user and makes the "guide" label honest, because the error source is known and measured. CADEngBench adds a pointed warning. Of 1,809 FEA attempts, CalculiX itself failed only **5 times**, but meshing or boundary-condition setup failed **573 times** and 554 runs solved yet disagreed with the reference ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09296v1)). Fixtures and loads should therefore be structured presets that agents select, not free-form inputs they author. Surrogates earn a place later, inside paid cloud studies. During a generative or parametric sweep on one part, train a small per-part model (a GP, or a light MeshGraphNet or Transolver) on the first N CalculiX runs, screen variants with it, and re-solve the Pareto front with the real solver. That keeps the surrogate inside its training distribution, which is the use the commercial vendors actually serve. Conformal intervals calibrated on held-out solver runs are the published way to flag where it is unsure, though the evidence comes from automotive CFD, not structural parts ([conformal UQ for surrogates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17297)). ML topology optimization follows the same rule. **TopoDiT (OAT2)** reports a **21.8% single-sample failure rate**, self-reported and with its weights licence unchecked ([OpenTO/TopoDiT](https://huggingface.co/OpenTO/TopoDiT)). It can warm-start a classical SIMP or level-set optimizer, but every candidate needs solver verification. Turning density fields back into editable features is a non-ML reconstruction problem, so the design's "rough or manual" rebuild matches the state of the art ([Feng et al., JCDE](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/12/9/162/8250036)).

Video and interactive world models are further away still. On IntPhys 2, V-JEPA 2 scores **0.52 against 96.44% for humans**, which is chance level ([arXiv 2512.06232](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06232)). On Physics-IQ, the best generative video model reached **29.5%** of the physical-variance baseline, and physical understanding was unrelated to visual realism ([Physics-IQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09038)). A June 2026 revision of the benchmark reshuffled the rankings, so vendor leaderboard claims such as NVIDIA's for Cosmos 3 depend on which version was used ([Physics-IQ Verified](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/physics-iq-verified--arxiv-2606.18943/)). Cosmos 3 is commercially licensed (OpenMDW 1.1) but outputs video and robot actions, never N, MPa or Hz ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-3-physical-ai-open-world-foundation-model/)). Genie 3 is a US consumer subscription with no developer API ([Project Genie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Genie_(website))). World Labs Marble needs its Pro plan for commercial rights and exports splats and collider meshes ([World Labs docs](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/marble/export/gaussian-splat.md)). Their only honest roles are UI backdrop and a low-value "does this look wrong?" critic that any vision LLM already provides. Rigid-body simulators are different: they compute real quantities, just not stress. **MuJoCo** (CPU, laptop-scale) and **Genesis** (official Apple Metal backend) can answer "does the phone stay in the cradle under a 2 g bump?" ([Genesis docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/overview/installation.html); [MuJoCo overview](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html)). The peak contact force from such a run is a better CalculiX boundary condition than a guessed load. Isaac Sim needs an RTX 4080 and does not run on macOS, which excludes it locally ([Isaac Sim requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/installation/requirements.html)).

Image- and text-to-3D generators make triangle meshes, and their own vendors call precision parts a weak spot ([Sloyd](https://www.sloyd.ai/llms/blog/how-accurate-are-ai-generated-3d-models-from-images.md)). The licences are also messy. **TRELLIS.2** is MIT, but its Apple Silicon port needs about 24 GB and pulls in a CC BY-NC background-removal model ([TRELLIS.2 Apple Silicon port](https://github.com/apetersson/TRELLIS.2-apple-silicon)). **Hunyuan3D**'s licence excludes the EU, the UK and South Korea ([triposr.org](https://triposr.org/blog/hunyuan3d-vs-trellis)). Meshy and Rodin are per-call SaaS. Even the best mesh-or-scan-to-CadQuery model reaches only **67.9% IoU on real scans** ([cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)), so the shape comes back roughly right with the dimensions wrong. The user's stated constraints (phone width, bolt pattern, cable slot) are the engineering content, and an agent writing parametric features supplies them. Generated meshes are worth supporting only as ghosted reference underlays in the existing "In context" mode. Scan-to-CAD of a mating object is a later, paid-tier feature that waits on a commercially licensed model.

## A pluggable backend seam keeps learned models from becoming evidence

The integration pattern is the same for every model. Implement it as an ordinary `mises` MCP tool, so every ACP agent gets it with no per-agent work. Give the tool a backend interface of `local_mlx | local_onnx | cloud`. The core chooses the backend: local by default, and cloud only after a server-side paid-licence check, charged to the per-user ledger. Results are typed and carry a file handle, following §7.4. Use MLX on macOS and ONNX Runtime or llama.cpp on Windows, and download weights on first use as §9 already plans for solvers. Hosted inference is cheap. At about **$0.80/h for an L4 on Modal** with per-second billing, a 10-second VLM call costs about $0.002 of GPU time ([Spheron on Modal pricing](https://www.spheron.network/blog/modal-gpu-pricing-2026-per-second-billing/), aggregator figure). The paid tier's real cost is hosted agent tokens, not auxiliary models.

The safety rule matters more than the plumbing. Every sim or estimate result should carry `{solver, version, fidelity_class, inputs_hash, validated_against}`, where `fidelity_class` is one of `fem`, `rigid_dynamics`, `learned_surrogate` or `learned_critic`. A "sim passed" badge should require `fem`, or a surrogate whose `validated_against` points to a FEM run on the same geometry. Learned-critic output always lands in the Step log as an unverified claim. That one field turns the anti-goal "agents reporting checks they didn't run" into something the server enforces, and it stays true whatever models arrive in 2027. Two more cheap seams belong in the design now. The first is a solver-neutral environment file: loads, fixtures, temperature, a vibration PSD or shock profile, gravity, named contact primitives, and an optional `scene_ref`. It feeds CalculiX today and MuJoCo or a surrogate later. The second is storing every sim run's mesh and `inputs_hash`, plus opt-in capture of approved merges and rejected proposals. That is the only clean training corpus Mises will ever have, for a sketch reader or a structural surrogate alike.

Evaluation closes the loop. Mises should run a nightly, offline regression suite on the local mirror with no Onshape calls. It applies the field's metric stack in order. First a validity gate. Then volume IoU (B-rep boolean, not voxels) plus Chamfer and bbox and mass tolerances. Then hole and feature recall plus keep-in and keep-out interfaces. Then editability, by perturbing each variable ±10–20% and counting rebuild failures. Then sim and slicer pass, and finally process cost (Onshape calls, tokens, approvals, stale-version replans) ([CADEngBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09296); [CADGenBench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)). Seed it with BenchCAD (CC-BY-4.0) and dogfood parts, then grow it from opt-in sessions. Gate every prompt and tool-description change on no regressions, and optionally submit "Mises + agent X" to CADGenBench for public credibility.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "better at CAD" means for Mises. Model quality is not the scarce resource. Frontier agents improve every few months at no cost to Mises, while the specialist models they compete with are mostly non-commercial and already behind. What is scarce is **trusted feedback**: exact measurements, honest simulation error bars, typed requirements, and a server that refuses to let pictures or estimates masquerade as checks. Every hour spent on the `mises` tool surface and its evidence rules compounds across Claude, Codex, Gemini and whatever ships next. A hosted CAD model would instead lock Mises to one vendor's weights and one licence. The defensible asset that follows is data, not models. Approved merges, rejected proposals, sketches and solver runs, captured with consent and stored as mirror geometry, form the one cleanly licensed CAD corpus Mises can own. That corpus is what eventually trains the two learned components with a real case: a small sketch reader and an in-distribution structural surrogate.

The open risks are specific and checkable. They are: whether Onshape FeatureScript MCP calls count against user allowances; the exact licences of Qwen3-VL, cadrille, CAD-Coder's training data and OAT2; real MLX memory and throughput on a 24 GB M5 Pro; and how current Claude, GPT and Gemini models score on CADEngBench and BenchCAD, since the papers tested Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.7. The trigger to revisit world models is concrete as well. Look again only when one publishes solver-validated predictions in physical units on a mechanical benchmark. Until then, they are scenery.
