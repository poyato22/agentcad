# World models and 3D generative models: are any useful for engineering CAD in Mises? (as of 2026-10-24)

Context: Mises (design doc §3, §6, §7) is a Tauri + Python desktop harness. Agents edit variable-driven Onshape parts, iterate on a local build123d/OCCT mirror, and check them with gmsh + CalculiX (static, thermal, modal) under preset environments (Desk, Outdoor, Bike mount, Car dashboard 80°C). Results are labelled "guide". Art/game mesh generation is a non-goal. Writing our own solvers is a non-goal. Presenting simulation as a guarantee is an anti-goal. Verdicts must come from server records, not agent prose (baseline README §4). Target hardware is an M5 Pro MacBook Pro with 24 GB. Research ran 2026-10-24 with about 16 search/fetch calls. Items marked "not verified this run" come from background knowledge and must be checked before use.

## Q1. World foundation models (Cosmos, Genie 3, V-JEPA 2, World Labs Marble, successors): what they model, and any credible path to mechanical design

### Takeaway
None of these models predicts stress, deflection, vibration response, fatigue or fit in engineering units. They model video, pixels and latent frames, robot actions, or explorable 3D scenes. Independent benchmarks still put their physical understanding far below real footage, and often at chance. For Mises they are at most a speculative, qualitative "does this look plausible" critic, and they can never be a source of verdicts. CalculiX and the OCCT checks stay the authority.

### Cited Findings
**NVIDIA Cosmos**
- The Cosmos platform launched at CES in January 2025 as "world foundation models" for physical AI: video generation and prediction for robotics and autonomous vehicles, initially under the NVIDIA Open Model License. — [NVIDIA newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-launches-cosmos-world-foundation-model-platform-to-accelerate-physical-ai-development); [Cosmos paper, arXiv 2501.03575](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.03575v1); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/06/nvidia-releases-its-own-brand-of-world-models)
- Cosmos 3 was announced 2026-05-31 at GTC Taipei/COMPUTEX. It is an "omnimodel" combining vision reasoning and generation across "text, video, images, ambient sound and action". It outputs "physically plausible video sequences" and numeric robot actions ("joint angles, gripper positions and trajectory points"). It is said to rank highly on Physics-IQ. The release names a "Cosmos 3 Nano" variant. — [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-3-physical-ai-open-world-foundation-model/)
- Cosmos 3 is licensed under the Linux Foundation's **OpenMDW 1.1**, which covers weights, code, data and benchmarks, and the models are on Hugging Face. NVIDIA's Cosmos page says the world foundation models are under OpenMDW 1.1. A third-party guide says the license permits commercial use with "Built on NVIDIA Cosmos" attribution. — [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-3-physical-ai-open-world-foundation-model/); [NVIDIA Cosmos page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/); [digitalapplied guide (secondary)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/nvidia-cosmos-3-open-physical-ai-omnimodel-2026-guide)
- The Cosmos 3 announcement does not mention CAD or engineering design. Simulation appears only as robot-policy testing (RoboLab). The blog gives no hardware requirements. — [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-3-physical-ai-open-world-foundation-model/)

**Google DeepMind Genie 3**
- Genie 3 was announced 2025-08-05. It generates interactive worlds at 720p and 24 fps in real time, stays consistent for "a few minutes", and keeps visual memory back to about one minute. — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)
- Stated limitations: "The range of actions agents can perform directly is currently constrained." Modelling multi-agent interactions is unsolved. Real locations are not geographically accurate. Text is unreliable. Sessions last "a few minutes ... rather than extended hours". — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)
- Access: Project Genie launched 2026-01-29 via Google Labs, US only, for Google AI Ultra subscribers. I found no general developer API. One secondary page still describes Genie 3 as partner-only, which appears out of date. — [Wikipedia: Project Genie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Genie_(website)); [Wikipedia: Genie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(world_model)); [CometAPI (older, conflicting)](https://www.cometapi.com/google-unveils-genie-3/)
- Waymo built a driving world model on Genie 3. — [Wikipedia: Waymo World Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo_World_Model)

**Meta V-JEPA 2**
- On IntPhys 2, the benchmark's authors report models "close to chance (50%) across all subsets", and V-JEPA 2 is among the models tested. — [IntPhys 2, arXiv 2506.09849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09849)
- An independent paper measures V-JEPA-2-H at 0.52 accuracy on IntPhys 2, against humans at 96.44%. — [arXiv 2512.06232](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06232)
- Secondary coverage of Meta's launch says V-JEPA 2 scored 44.5% paired accuracy on MVPBench, the best of the systems tested, and highlights its weakness at long-horizon planning and causal reasoning. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/metas-latest-model-highlights-the-challenge-ai-faces-in-long-term-planning-and-causal-reasoning/)

**Physics benchmarks for generative video**
- Physics-IQ (396 real videos; WACV 2026): the best model reached only 29.5% of the physical-variance baseline, where real video scores 100%. "No category" counts as solved. The authors also found physical understanding to be unrelated to visual realism. — [arXiv 2501.09038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09038); [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Motamed_Do_Generative_Video_Models_Understand_Physical_Principles_WACV_2026_paper.html)
- Physics-IQ Verified (June 2026) refined 57.6% of samples, and model rankings moved (Kendall τ = 0.46). Leaderboard claims, including NVIDIA's Physics-IQ claim for Cosmos 3, are therefore sensitive to which benchmark version was used. — [arXiv 2606.18943 summary](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/physics-iq-verified--arxiv-2606.18943/)

**World Labs (Marble, RTFM, World API)**
- RTFM, a real-time interactive video world model, was a research preview on 2025-10-16. Marble went public on 2025-11-12, the public World API launched on 2026-01-21 (prepaid credits), and Marble 1.1 shipped on 2026-04-02. — [World Labs: World API](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/announcing-the-world-api); [NYU Shanghai RITS (Marble 1.1)](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/world-labs-releases-marble-1-1-auto-expanding-3d-world-generation)
- Marble exports Gaussian splats (SPZ, PLY), 360° panoramas and collider meshes on the Standard plan. A high-quality textured GLB mesh and commercial rights require the Pro plan. The free plan cannot export. — [World Labs docs](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/marble/export/gaussian-splat.md)
- Third-party pages put the collider mesh at about 100k–200k triangles with no UVs. — [skills.cat (third-party)](https://skills.cat/skills/cloudai-x/world-labs-skills/world-labs-export)
- Unverified: one page claims AMD agreed in September 2026 to acquire World Labs for about $8.2B. Other sources say only that World Labs raised $1B in February 2026 from NVIDIA, AMD and Autodesk. — [yespress (uncorroborated)](https://yespress.io/world-labs.md); [metavert](https://metavert.io/world-labs-vs-ami-labs)

**Engineering surrogates, the field adjacent to world models**
- NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo trains surrogates (MeshGraphNet, Transolver) on data from solvers such as LS-DYNA and OpenRadioss to predict deformation fields. NVIDIA itself keeps "high-fidelity solver validation in the loop", and the release notes include a small-deformation structural sample. — [NVIDIA structural analysis](https://www.nvidia.com/en-au/use-cases/structural-analysis/); [PhysicsNeMo release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/physicsnemo/26.03/release-notes/index.html)

### Inferences
- **What they model versus what Mises needs.** Cosmos, Genie and RTFM predict appearance over time. V-JEPA predicts latent video features. Marble produces static scene geometry. A bike mount's real failure modes, such as resonance near road-vibration frequencies, layer delamination or creep at 80°C, cannot be seen in RGB video at the scale and time span these models handle. No source showed any of them outputting a quantity in N, MPa or Hz. Using them for load or vibration prediction is **speculative with no evidence**, and would breach the "simulation as guarantee" anti-goal if shown as a result.
- **The only plausible near-term role is a qualitative critic.** A vision-language reasoner (for example Cosmos 3's reasoning side) could be shown CalculiX renders or an assembly animation and asked "does anything look physically wrong?". Any LLM with vision already does this. Its output must be labelled an *unverified claim* (baseline README §4) and must never gate approval. Expected value is low, because chance-level results on IntPhys 2 suggest it would mostly add noise.
- **Assembly and fit reasoning.** Mises already does this exactly with OCCT interference and distance checks (§7.2). A learned model adds nothing to a boolean that is already correct.
- **Environment context is the most realistic indirect use.** A Marble or Genie scene could give the user a visual backdrop ("the bike"), or a collider mesh to place a part in. That is UI flavour. It adds no engineering signal.
- **Licensing.** Cosmos 3 (OpenMDW 1.1) is the only one in this group that is commercially redistributable. Genie 3 is a consumer subscription with no API. Marble requires the Pro plan for commercial rights. V-JEPA 2 license: see Gaps.
- **Hardware.** Cosmos generation models are designed for NVIDIA datacentre and RTX GPUs. Running them on a 24 GB M5 Pro is unlikely to be practical, except perhaps a Nano reasoning variant through an MLX/llama.cpp-style port, which is not verified. Genie and Marble are cloud-only.

### Gaps
- No source gives Cosmos 3 parameter counts, VRAM needs or Apple Silicon support. The full OpenMDW 1.1 terms were not read; read the model card before shipping.
- The V-JEPA 2 code and weights license was not verified this run. Background knowledge says it was released openly in June 2025, but check the GitHub/Hugging Face license.
- I found no paper, demo or credible claim of a world model used for structural, vibration or fit prediction of mechanical parts. Absence of evidence is itself the finding.
- 2025–2026 successors I did not research: Genie 4 (if it exists), Meta V-JEPA successors, Tencent/Alibaba world models, Odyssey, Decart, Runway GWM. They may matter for the 2027–2028 view.

## Q2. 3D asset generators (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D, Meshy, Rodin, TripoSR) and mesh-to-CAD: a usable starting point for parts?

### Takeaway
Image/text-to-3D generators produce plausible *triangle meshes*, not dimensioned, feature-based solids. Vendors themselves call precision parts a weak spot. Mesh/point-to-CAD research moved fast in 2025–2026 (cadrille, ParaCAD, PrimitiveCAD). The most promising line generates **CadQuery/Python code**, which fits Mises's build123d mirror. But it works on simple extrude-dominated shapes, reaches only about 68% IoU on real scans, and none of it emits Onshape feature trees. For "a phone cradle shaped like X", a generated mesh is at best a visual or reference underlay. The real part should come from an agent writing parametric features.

### Cited Findings
**Generators**
- TRELLIS.2 model and code are MIT-licensed. Its rendering dependencies nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec have separate NVIDIA terms. — [codesota](https://www.codesota.com/news/trellis-2-3d-generation); [triposr.org comparison](https://triposr.org/blog/hunyuan3d-vs-trellis)
- A community Apple Silicon port of TRELLIS.2 exists. One review says it needs "24GB of unified memory, minimum", drops texture export and disables hole filling. Its dependencies include DINOv3 (Meta custom gated license) and RMBG-2.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial). — [GitHub: TRELLIS.2-apple-silicon](https://github.com/apetersson/TRELLIS.2-apple-silicon); [Agent Wars](https://www.agent-wars.com/news/2025-04-20-trellis-2-image-to-3d-apple-silicon-port); [swiftwand comparison](https://swiftwand.com/en/open-source-3d-generation-trellis-hunyuan-2026-en/)
- Hunyuan3D 2.1 weights use the Tencent Hunyuan 3D Community License. It caps use at 1M MAU, excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, and forbids training non-Hunyuan models on outputs. An MLX port exists (MIT code, Tencent weights). — [triposr.org](https://triposr.org/blog/hunyuan3d-vs-trellis); [trify3d](https://trify3d.com/blog/trellis-2-vs-hunyuan-3d)
- An Apache-2.0 local Apple Silicon/NVIDIA pipeline (October 2026) bundles Pixal3D, TRELLIS.2, Hunyuan3D and SF3D. It targets game characters (rigging, animation), which shows where this ecosystem is aimed. — [GitHub: image-to-3dlab](https://github.com/Bingeljell/image-to-3dlab)
- Meshy 7 is vendor-benchmarked at 81.0% "overall proportion", 79.7% spatial distribution and 59.8% surface detail. These are image-alignment scores, not dimensional tolerances. — [All3DP](https://all3dp.com/4/meshy-7-wants-to-put-an-end-to-five-legged-unicorns/)
- Vendors say "precision engineering parts" produce "blobby" artifacts, and describe image-to-3D as "generative interpretation", not measurement. A guide on 2D-to-print workflows advises doing final precision modelling in CAD. — [Sloyd](https://www.sloyd.ai/llms/blog/how-accurate-are-ai-generated-3d-models-from-images.md); [Sloyd: photos](https://www.sloyd.ai/llms/blog/3d-models-from-photos-quality-accuracy-and-limits.md); [cadmore](https://cadmore.com/blog/convert-an-image-to-3d)

**Mesh / point cloud → CAD**
- **cadrille** (arXiv 2505.22914, v3 February 2026) maps point clouds (256 points), 4-view images or text to **executable CadQuery Python**. It is built on Qwen2-VL-2B, with SFT on the roughly 1M-program procedural CAD-Recode set, then RL with an IoU-minus-invalidity reward. — [arXiv 2505.22914](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)
- cadrille on real scans (CC3D, 2,973 noisy scans): RL version CD 0.47, **IoU 67.9%**, invalid outputs 0.2%, against CAD-Recode at IoU 60.5% and 9.8% invalid. Multi-view images on CC3D: IoU 65.0%. Code is at github.com/col14m/cadrille, license not stated in the paper. — [arXiv 2505.22914](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3)
- **ParaCAD** (arXiv 2607.17093, July 2026) does point-cloud-conditioned autoregressive B-rep generation with exact surface types. It claims better precision and watertightness than point-based baselines, but the abstract gives no numbers and no code. It outputs a B-rep with explicit surfaces, not an editable feature history. — [arXiv 2607.17093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17093)
- **PrimitiveCAD** (arXiv 2610.08698, October 2026) is an LLM that turns a point cloud into an executable Python script for a parametric B-rep, with primitive-aware tokens and RL. Related 2026 work includes CADENA (stepwise reverse engineering, arXiv 2608.00799), Pointer-CAD (arXiv 2603.04337), CADFit (mesh-to-CAD programs, cited as ICML 2026) and Mesh2brep (TVCG 2025, primitive fitting). I did not verify their results. — [PrimitiveCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08698); [CADENA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00799); [P2CADNet (older baseline)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02638)

### Inferences
- **The pipeline that could work** is: user photo or sketch → (optional) generated mesh → point/mesh-to-CadQuery model (cadrille-class) → agent ports it to build123d → agent rewrites it as Onshape features with named variables. Every arrow loses information. A 68% IoU on real scans means the shape is roughly right while the dimensions are wrong, so the agent must re-dimension from user-stated constraints (phone width, cable slot, mount bolt pattern) anyway. Those constraints *are* the engineering content. The generated shape adds only styling.
- **Where it actually helps:** (a) a mood/reference mesh shown ghosted in the viewer next to the parametric part, as in the existing "In context" mode. (b) Reverse-engineering an *existing* physical object the part must mate to, such as a phone scan or a handlebar, which is where scan-to-CAD research is aimed. (c) M9 sketch-to-part, where a cadrille-style multi-view → CadQuery model is a closer fit than any mesh generator.
- **Licensing for a shipped product.** TRELLIS.2 (MIT) is the only clean option, and the Mac port's non-commercial background-removal dependency must be swapped out. Hunyuan3D's EU/UK exclusion probably rules it out for a global desktop app. Meshy and Rodin are SaaS with per-call cost, which conflicts with the "free tier costs us nothing" anti-goal unless users bring their own key.
- **Hardware.** 24 GB is reported as the floor for the TRELLIS.2 Mac port, so it would compete with the agent host, OCCT and CalculiX for memory. Run it on demand only, or remotely.

### Gaps
- I found no independent dimensional-accuracy benchmark (mm error, wall thickness) for TRELLIS.2, Hunyuan3D 3.x, Meshy, Rodin or TripoSR. Rodin and TripoSR were not researched in depth. Hunyuan3D 3.0 license terms were not found.
- cadrille's code license and real-world performance on multi-feature parts (fillets, shells, patterns, threads) are unknown. CC3D numbers are aggregates.
- Point2CAD and classic NURBS-fitting / scan-to-CAD tools (Geomagic Design X, QuickSurface, Fusion mesh-to-BRep) were not researched this run.

## Q3. Robotics/embodied simulators (Isaac Sim, MuJoCo, Genesis) as environment testers for printed parts; laptop feasibility

### Takeaway
These are rigid/soft-contact dynamics engines for robot learning. They can answer kinematic and contact questions, such as "does the phone stay in the cradle under 2 g bumps?" or "does this clip snap over the bar?". They cannot answer stress or failure questions, which stay with CalculiX. On an M5 Pro, Isaac Sim is impossible. Genesis has official Metal support, and MuJoCo is the lightest, most stable option. A MuJoCo/Genesis "shake test" tool is a feasible, honest add-on, provided its results are labelled as a guide.

### Cited Findings
- Isaac Sim 5.x supports only Ubuntu 22.04/24.04 and Windows 10/11. Its minimum GPU is an RTX 4080 (recommended RTX 5080), with 32 GB RAM. The only aarch64 builds are for DGX Spark. There is no macOS. — [Isaac Sim 5.1 requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/installation/requirements.html); [Isaac Sim 6.0 requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/6.0.0/installation/requirements.html); [Isaac Lab install](https://docs.nvidia.com/isaaclab/2.3/isaac-lab/local-installation)
- The Genesis installation docs list an Apple Silicon Metal backend (`gs.metal`), with GPU simulation, CPU simulation, viewer and headless rendering all marked supported on macOS. — [Genesis docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/overview/installation.html)
- Genesis launched in December 2024 with very large speed claims ("430,000x faster than real-time") that come from social and marketing posts. — [Philipp Schmid on X](https://x.com/_philschmid/status/1869639246434246966); [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2024/12/19/meet-genesis-an-open-source-physics-ai-engine-redefining-robotics-with-ultra-fast-simulations-and-generative-4d-worlds/)
- MuJoCo simulates constrained rigid bodies with soft contacts. Its "flexes" are 1D/2D/3D deformable meshes with collisions. Flex elements produce forces that "softly preserve the shape". The overview describes no FEM stress output. — [MuJoCo overview](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html)

### Inferences
- **A useful, bounded use case.** Export the mirror part and its environment fixtures (handlebar cylinder, phone box, mass properties from Onshape) to MJCF or Genesis. Apply a vibration or shock profile taken from the environment preset, and report whether contact is kept, how far parts slip or rotate, and the peak contact force. A peak contact force taken from rigid-body sim would be a *better boundary condition* for the CalculiX static run than a guessed load. This couples dynamics to FEM without either solver pretending to be the other. It is consistent with "we wrap existing solvers".
- **Limits that must be shown to users.** Rigid contact with tuned softness parameters gives answers that are sensitive to those parameters. Snap-fit strain, creep and layer delamination are out of scope. Label every result as a "guide" with solver, timestep and contact parameters, per §7.5.
- **Feasibility on the laptop.** MuJoCo on CPU is easily laptop-scale for single-part tests. Genesis on Metal is plausible but younger, and claims about it should be benchmarked locally. Isaac Sim is excluded locally. It could only be a paid cloud study on an NVIDIA instance, which offers little over MuJoCo for a single printed part.

### Gaps
- MuJoCo's macOS/Apple Silicon support and its Apache-2.0 license were not confirmed in a fetched source this run. Background knowledge says both hold. Check the MuJoCo GitHub releases and LICENSE.
- MuJoCo Warp / MJX on Apple Silicon, Genesis FEM/MPM solver maturity, and the Genesis license were not verified.
- I found no published example of using these simulators to validate a 3D-printed consumer part.

## Q4. Where world models are heading (2026–2028), and what Mises should design for now

### Takeaway
The trend is clear: open-weight physical-AI omnimodels (Cosmos 3 under OpenMDW), commercial world-generation APIs (World Labs World API, January 2026), consumer interactive worlds (Project Genie, January 2026), and learned engineering surrogates (PhysicsNeMo). Credible engineering usefulness is likely to come from **surrogates trained on solver data**, not from video world models. Mises should not integrate any world model now. It should build three cheap seams so one can be added later without breaking its verdict rules.

### Cited Findings
- Release timeline: Cosmos (January 2025) → Cosmos 3 omnimodel with action output (May 2026). Genie 3 (August 2025) → Project Genie consumer access (January 2026) → Waymo variant. RTFM (October 2025) → Marble (November 2025) → World API (January 2026) → Marble 1.1 (April 2026). — [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-3-physical-ai-open-world-foundation-model/); [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/); [Wikipedia: Project Genie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Genie_(website)); [World Labs](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/announcing-the-world-api)
- Benchmarks are being audited and revised (Physics-IQ Verified, June 2026). New 2026 benchmarks focus on long-horizon stability and multi-turn interaction of interactive world models (WorldRoamBench, WBench), not physical quantities. — [Physics-IQ Verified](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/physics-iq-verified--arxiv-2606.18943/); [WorldRoamBench, arXiv 2606.31672](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31672); [WBench, arXiv 2605.25874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25874)
- Learned CAE surrogates are trained from conventional solver outputs, and vendors keep the solver in the loop for validation. — [NVIDIA structural analysis](https://www.nvidia.com/en-au/use-cases/structural-analysis/)
- CAD-as-code reconstruction (point cloud, images or text → CadQuery/Python B-rep) is the most active 2026 research line bridging generative models and CAD. — [cadrille](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22914v3); [PrimitiveCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08698)

### Inferences (design recommendations; speculative where noted)
1. **A `sim` provider interface, not a hard-wired solver.** Give `sim.run` a `solver` field (`calculix`, later `mujoco_dynamics`, `surrogate:<name>`). Each result carries `{solver, version, fidelity_class: "fem" | "rigid_dynamics" | "learned_surrogate" | "learned_critic", inputs_hash, validated_against}`. Verdict badges should require `fidelity_class ∈ {fem}`, or a surrogate whose `validated_against` points to a FEM run on the same geometry. This keeps future learned models from ever becoming the evidence behind "sim passed".
2. **Write environment presets as a structured, solver-neutral file now.** Include temperature, UV, load cases and fixtures for FEM, plus a vibration PSD or shock profile, gravity orientation, contact partners as named primitives/meshes (handlebar Ø, phone box), and an optional `scene_ref` (glTF/USD/splat) for visuals. The same file feeds CalculiX today, MuJoCo/Genesis next, and a world model or surrogate later. A USD or glTF scene reference is the common currency of Isaac, Cosmos, Marble and Genesis, which is why `scene_ref` is worth having (the format claim is inference, not verified per tool).
3. **Reserve MCP tool slots, behind feature flags and classed as `read`/`local_mutate`:** `critic.physical_review(render_handles) → unverified notes`, `env.scene_from_image(image) → scene_ref` (World API, BYO key), `ref.mesh_from_image(image) → ghost mesh handle`, and `ref.cad_from_scan(points|mesh) → build123d script draft`. All outputs are *drafts or unverified claims* in the step log, which matches baseline README §4.
4. **Most likely to pay off by 2028 (speculative):** a learned structural surrogate for fast "live" previews (§7.5's coarse-then-refine loop), fine-tuned on Mises's own CalculiX runs. That would need opt-in telemetry of geometry, environment and results. Storing every sim run with `inputs_hash` and the mesh now keeps that option open at almost no cost.
5. **Do not** build any of this into v1. Video world models (Cosmos, Genie, RTFM) are not on the critical path for engineering geometry. Re-evaluate when a model publishes quantitative, solver-validated predictions in physical units on mechanical benchmarks.

### Gaps
- I found no source forecasting world models for mechanical/structural engineering specifically. The 2026–2028 direction above is inference from release cadence and adjacent CAE-surrogate work.
- I did not research whether Onshape, Autodesk or PTC have announced world-model or surrogate integrations in 2026. Autodesk's reported investment in World Labs hints at interest, but its stated purpose was not found. — [metavert](https://metavert.io/world-labs-vs-ami-labs)
- No MCP or other standard for "environment description" exchange between simulators and world models was found. USD/MJCF/URDF exist, but cross-tool convergence is unverified.
